## 中文内容概述（约 300 字）

理查德·罗兹的《原子弹的制造》追踪核裂变从早期物理发现到曼哈顿计划和1945年原子弹投放的历史。作者不仅介绍科学家如何理解原子核结构、链式反应和临界质量，也描写纳粹德国的威胁、美国政府的决策、工业动员、实验设施和战争伦理。作品将科学史、技术史、军事史和人物传记结合起来，展示理论知识如何在国家资源、组织管理和工程实践中转化为武器。罗兹关注奥本海默、费米、西拉德等科学家，也讨论政治领导人和军方如何决定项目方向。原子弹的成功并不只是某个天才的发现，而是跨学科研究、巨大工业体系和战争压力共同作用的结果。书中也提出科学家在战争中的责任、核武器对平民的影响以及核时代的安全困境。阅读时应区分科学原理解释、历史叙述和作者的伦理评价，并结合后续核史研究理解广岛、长崎及战后核政治。

## English Summary (approximately 300 words)

Richard Rhodes’s *The Making of the Atomic Bomb* traces the development of nuclear fission from early discoveries in physics to the Manhattan Project and the use of atomic weapons in 1945. The book explains the scientific breakthroughs that made a chain reaction possible, including the work of physicists who investigated the structure of the atom, neutron behavior, uranium, plutonium, and critical mass. It also follows the political and military circumstances that transformed these discoveries into a vast wartime engineering project.

Rhodes connects scientific history with the rise of Nazi Germany, the fear that the Axis powers might develop nuclear weapons, the mobilization of American industry, and the creation of secret laboratories and production facilities. The work discusses scientists such as Enrico Fermi, Leo Szilard, and J. Robert Oppenheimer, while also examining the roles of political leaders, military officials, engineers, and industrial organizations. The bomb was not the achievement of one isolated genius. It depended on international scientific knowledge, large-scale funding, coordinated management, and the labor of thousands of people.

The narrative also addresses the ethical consequences of scientific work during wartime. The decision to build and use the bomb raises questions about civilian casualties, military necessity, scientific responsibility, and the security dilemmas that followed the beginning of the nuclear age. Rhodes does not treat the technical achievement as separate from its human and political consequences.

Published in 1986, the book won the Pulitzer Prize for General Nonfiction and is widely regarded as a major work of science and technology history. It is valuable for understanding nuclear physics at an accessible level, the relationship between science and the state, industrial research, and the moral responsibilities of scientists. Readers should combine it with later scholarship on Hiroshima, Nagasaki, and postwar nuclear policy for a broader account.

## 核心学习价值

- 理解核裂变、链式反应和核武器研发的基本背景。
- 认识曼哈顿计划的工业、组织和跨学科协作。
- 分析科学、国家安全和战争决策的关系。
- 讨论科学家责任、平民伤亡与核时代伦理。
- 结合科学原理与历史证据理解技术发展。
